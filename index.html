Connect 4 Strategy

Eleven short lessons, from your first double threat to the theory that solved the game.

This course was written for a parent who wanted two things at once: to reliably win — or lose gracefully, on purpose — against a kid across the table, and to actually understand why Connect 4 is a solved game rather than take it on faith. Both threads run through every lesson. Each one is short (5–10 minutes), teaches one idea, and proves it with an interactive, machine-verified puzzle rather than a diagram you have to trust.

Every non-obvious claim is either backed by a citation or, where no good source existed, verified programmatically before publishing — board positions checked for legality and uniqueness, opening evaluations queried live from a perfect solver. Read the glossary first if a term stops you.

The lessons

Built to be read in order — each one leans on the last — but if you already play casually, start wherever the description grabs you.

  1. The Double Threat — the one idea behind almost every won game: two ways to win at once.
  2. The "7" Trap — a five-disc shape that makes blocking suicidal.
  3. Odd and Even Threats — why a winning square only counts if it falls on your row.
  4. Zugzwang — an interactive endgame simulator: win by forcing your opponent to move.
  5. Opening Play — why the first move is d, and how to punish every common reply (solver-verified).
  6. Defense — the five-question scan to run before every move.
  7. Coaching Your Kid — staging discoveries and honest handicaps, grounded in guided-play research.
  8. Threats That Share a Column — the "basement rule": when claims stack up, only the lowest one is real.
  9. Allis's Nine Rules — the actual certificate system behind the 1988 proof, read from the source thesis.
  10. The Trap Zoo — the "7" was one animal in a family; a recognition gallery for the rest.
  11. Playing Second — what to do when you don't get to open.

Sources

Primary sources cited throughout: Victor Allis's 1988 thesis A Knowledge-based Approach of Connect-Four (the proof that the first player wins); Pascal Pons' perfect solver (used live for every opening and endgame evaluation in Lessons 5, 6, and 11); and, for the coaching thread, a meta-analysis of guided-play research and general chess-teaching craft, both linked from Lesson 7.

How this was built: this course was authored interactively with Claude (Anthropic), working from primary sources rather than parametric memory — every board position was constructed and checked by a small verification script, and every opening claim was pulled live from the solver above. If a lesson's puzzle is unclear or a claim looks wrong, an AI assistant can trace the position or re-query the sources with you; that's how this was made.
