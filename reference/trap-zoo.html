The Trap Zoo

One skeleton, many bodies: two of your lines whose empty finishing squares stack in one column. Every specimen below is machine-verified. From Lesson 10; mechanism in Lesson 2 and Lesson 8.

The universal test

Trace each three-disc line to its empty finishing square. Same column, stacked → real trap (poisoned column). One shared square → a single claim in costume; one block kills it. Different columns → not a trap; audit each claim: alive → lowest → parity.

1 · The Seven (row + rising diagonal, sharing the corner disc)

2 · The Rails (two parallel diagonals, one row apart)

3 · The Hook (row + diagonal descending onto the stack from above)

4 · The X (rising and descending diagonals, crossing)

⚠ The Mirage (two lines, ONE finishing square — not a trap)

Field notes: kids build the rails by accident (diagonal walls are fun). The hook and X hide best — nothing sits on a shared corner disc, so nothing "looks like a 7". The mirage fools both sides: defenders panic, attackers overinvest — check the finishing squares before you do either. Verticals never join the zoo: their finishing square is always immediately playable, so they can't wait in ambush.
