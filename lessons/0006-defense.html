Warm-up (20 seconds, from Lesson 5)

The five-question scan

Good defense isn't a mood ("play carefully") — it's a fixed scan you run every single move, in this order, before your disc leaves your hand:

1. Can I win right now? Take it. (Yes, people miss these.)
2. Can they win right now? Block it — this outranks everything below.
3. Does my move hand them a win on top? Look at the square above where your disc lands. If it completes anything of theirs, the move is a gift. (Lesson 2's poison, seen from below.)
4. Are they building? Pairs with two open ends (Lesson 1), a 7 taking shape (Lesson 2). Kill structure while it's cheap.
5. Is the threat even real? Check the window isn't already dead (your disc in it) and check the claim's parity (Lesson 3). Blocking dead or wrong-parity threats wastes the tempo that loses endgames.

Questions 1–2 are reflexes you have. The drills below train 3, 4, and 5 — including the two ways a "safe-looking block" quietly costs you a game you'd already won. Take it to the living room

Run the scan out loud once with your kid — "can I win? can you win? am I giving you anything? what are you building? is it real?" — then never again; let them catch you skipping steps. That's coaching gold. And note what the scan does to your handicap toolkit: skipping question 4 on purpose (never killing their structure early, only ever blocking completed threes) makes you a genuinely beatable opponent who never looks like they're trying to lose.
