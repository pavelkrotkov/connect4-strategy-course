Warm-up (15 seconds, from Lesson 6)

The idea: be their zone, not their wall

You've been learning inside a principle this whole course: every lesson sat just past what you could already do. That's Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, and your kid needs the same thing from you at the board: an opponent exactly one notch stronger than their current game. (ZPD overview)

Two facts carry the whole lesson. First, a research finding: on the skills where it has been measured, guided discovery beats telling — and it also beats letting them flounder. A well-timed question tends to outperform both the lecture and the shrug. (meta-analysis of guided play, echoing Alfieri et al. 2011) Second, a piece of chess-parenting folklore that every parent who has tried it confirms: kids detect thrown games. A fake blunder teaches nothing and costs trust. The honest alternative is what you've been collecting since Lesson 3: real strategies that are genuinely weaker — c/e openings, even-parity claims, a skipped scan question — played at full effort. You can truly lose from those stances. That's what makes the wins they take from you real.

Take it to the living room

This week, play one game where you don't try to win or lose — just diagnose. Which stage on the card is your kid actually at? (Parents guess high; the tell is pairs, not threes.) Then set your notch: one stage up, honest handicap from the menu, one question per game. The finish line of this whole course isn't beating them — it's the day they run the five-question scan on you, find the seam, and win for real. Budget a few months. Enjoy losing ground.
